ICML 2026 Oral

Position: There are futures that benchmark driven AI cannot see

Ava Iranmanesh, Sobhan Lotfi, Lachin Naghashyar, Ali Shirali, Fateme Nateghi Haredasht, Sanmi Koyejo, Philip Torr, Yong Suk Lee, Fazl Barez, Joel Lehman, Peter Norvig, Arvind Narayanan

Benchmarks decide what counts as progress. We argue that a single dominant evaluation culture quietly taxes the ideas that do not fit today's metrics, and that the blind spots this creates are structural. More benchmarks will not fix it; plural evaluation regimes, protected venues for work that cannot be ranked on a leaderboard, and funding on longer horizons might.

CSCW 2026 Positech Workshop

Smolder: Empathy, Collapse, and What Remains for AI Risk

Ava Iranmanesh, Daryl Cameron

Empathy is tuned for threats that are near, specific, and familiar, which leaves the abstract and large scale risks of advanced AI almost invisible to it. I argue that this failure is where the alignment problem begins on the human side, and that compassion, rather than empathy, is the more realistic route to public engagement with AI risk.
